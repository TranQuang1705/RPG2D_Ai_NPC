# Presentation Script - Problem Statement Slide
## Slide 4: Current Limitations in RPG Games

**Total Speaking Time:** 2-3 minutes  
**Delivery Style:** Conversational, engaging, relatable

## OPENING STATEMENT (15 seconds)

**Script:**
"Before diving into our solution, let's examine the current state of NPC interactions in modern RPG games. Today's RPG experiences face four critical limitations that significantly impact player immersion and engagement."

**Delivery Tips:**
- Pause briefly after this statement
- Make eye contact with audience
- Use hand gesture to indicate you're about to list problems
- Show slight concern in tone to set up problem framing

---

## LIMITATION 1: Pre-scripted Dialogue Trees (30-40 seconds)

### 🎮 Pre-scripted dialogue trees → No natural conversation

**Script:**
"First, let's talk about dialogue systems. In most RPG games today, when you approach an NPC and want to have a conversation, you're presented with a menu of pre-written options. **[gesture clicking motion]** You might see choices like 'Tell me about the quest,' 'What can you sell me?' or 'Goodbye.' 

The problem here is twofold: **One**, you can only say what the developers programmed into those four or five buttons. If you want to ask something slightly different or more specific, you simply can't. **Two**, every player has the exact same conversation. There's no personalization, no spontaneity, no natural flow.

Imagine talking to a real person but only being allowed to choose from a list of five sentences. **[pause]** That's not a conversation—that's filling out a multiple-choice questionnaire. This breaks immersion and makes NPCs feel robotic rather than human-like."

**Delivery Tips:**
- Use hand gestures to "click" invisible buttons when mentioning menu options
- Emphasize "One" and "Two" with fingers
- Slow down at "multiple-choice questionnaire" for impact
- Show slight frustration in tone to convey the problem
- Make the "real person" analogy relatable

**Visual Aid Suggestion:**
Show a screenshot of traditional dialogue tree from popular RPG (Skyrim, Dragon Age, etc.)

---

## LIMITATION 2: Static NPC Behaviors (35-45 seconds)

### 🤖 Static NPC behaviors → Artificial, non-living world

**Script:**
"The second major limitation is static NPC behavior. Walk into a village in a typical RPG, and you'll notice something odd: NPCs are just... standing there. **[pause, stand still for effect]** Waiting. Waiting for you to click on them. 

They might have a basic idle animation—maybe they're sweeping the same spot for eight hours straight **[chuckle]**—but they don't have real lives. They don't sleep, they don't eat, they don't go to work or socialize with other NPCs. They exist solely as what we call 'quest vending machines.'

This creates what I call the 'museum effect.' The game world feels like a museum exhibit where everything is frozen in place until you, the visitor, interact with it. Real worlds don't work that way. In a living, breathing world, people have routines, schedules, and activities independent of whether the player is watching them or not.

When NPCs lack autonomy, the entire game world feels artificial and predictable, significantly reducing player immersion."

**Delivery Tips:**
- Actually stand still briefly when saying "standing there" for comedic/dramatic effect
- Laugh lightly at the "sweeping" example to show absurdity
- Use air quotes when saying "quest vending machines"
- Emphasize "museum effect" as a key concept
- Contrast "museum" vs "living world" clearly
- Slow down on final sentence for emphasis

**Visual Aid Suggestion:**
Show side-by-side comparison: static NPC (left) vs. your NPC with daily routine visualization (right)

---

## LIMITATION 3: Disconnected Game Systems (30-40 seconds)

### ⚙️ Disconnected game systems → Inconsistent context

**Script:**
"Third, we have the problem of disconnected systems. Most RPG games are built with separate systems for dialogue, quests, trading, inventory, and time management. While each system works fine individually, they don't communicate with each other effectively.

Here's a concrete example: You talk to an NPC merchant at midnight in the game. Realistically, they should say, 'I'm closed, come back during business hours.' But instead, their shop menu opens instantly because the dialogue system doesn't know what the time system is doing. **[gesture disconnect with hands]**

Or another scenario: An NPC asks you to collect ten flowers. You already have eight flowers in your inventory. But the NPC still says, 'You'll need to find ten flowers'—completely unaware of your inventory state.

This lack of context awareness breaks immersion. NPCs should understand what time it is, what quests you have, what items you're carrying, and adjust their responses accordingly. When systems work in silos, you get these jarring, inconsistent experiences."

**Delivery Tips:**
- Use hand gestures to show "separation" between systems
- Emphasize specific examples with vocal variety
- Use "air quotes" for dialogue examples
- Gesture "disconnect" by separating hands when mentioning system isolation
- Speed up slightly on examples to show frustration
- Slow down on "context awareness" to emphasize solution

**Visual Aid Suggestion:**
Show diagram of disconnected systems with X marks, then show your integrated architecture

---

## LIMITATION 4: No Voice Interaction Support (25-35 seconds)

### 💬 No voice interaction support → Limited accessibility

**Script:**
"Finally, let's address voice interaction—or rather, the lack of it. We live in an era where you can say 'Hey Siri' or 'Alexa' and have natural conversations with your devices. **[gesture speaking to phone]** Voice assistants are everywhere: our phones, our cars, our homes.

Yet in gaming, one of the most immersive entertainment mediums, we're still clicking buttons and typing text to communicate with characters. This is not only outdated from a technology standpoint, but it also limits accessibility for players with motor disabilities who may find keyboard or controller inputs challenging.

More importantly, voice is the most natural form of human communication. When you remove voice from character interactions, you're eliminating one of the most powerful tools for creating emotional connections and immersive experiences.

The question becomes: If we can talk naturally to our refrigerators, **[pause for effect]** why can't we talk naturally to characters in game worlds?"

**Delivery Tips:**
- Gesture talking to an invisible smart speaker/phone
- Show genuine curiosity when mentioning "outdated"
- Emphasize "accessibility" with sincere tone
- Pause after "refrigerators" for audience to absorb the irony
- End with rhetorical question, making eye contact with audience
- Let the question hang in the air for 2 seconds

**Visual Aid Suggestion:**
Show icons of Siri, Alexa, Google Assistant vs. keyboard/mouse/controller icons

## TRANSITION TO RESULT (15-20 seconds)

### Result: Reduced immersion & predictable gameplay

**Script:**
"So what's the cumulative effect of all these limitations? **[gesture bringing it together]**

Players experience reduced immersion because conversations feel scripted, worlds feel artificial, systems don't communicate, and interactions require outdated input methods. The result is predictable, repetitive gameplay that fails to emotionally engage players in meaningful ways.

**[confident tone shift]** This is the problem space we set out to address with Alex The Wanderer."

**Delivery Tips:**
- Gesture with hands coming together to show "cumulative"
- List the four problems quickly in succession
- Shift to confident, solution-oriented tone on last sentence
- Pause for 2 seconds before advancing to next slide
- Maintain eye contact during pause

## BACKUP RESPONSES FOR Q&A

### If asked: "Don't some games already have dynamic NPCs?"

**Response:**
"That's an excellent question. Yes, some AAA games like Red Dead Redemption 2 or The Witcher 3 have NPCs with basic routines—walking between points, day/night cycles. However, these are still scripted paths and animations. They don't dynamically respond to player requests, don't have voice-driven interactions, and their dialogue systems remain menu-based. Our innovation combines autonomous routines WITH natural language processing AND player request overrides, creating a more flexible, interactive system. We'll see this in action during the demo."

### If asked: "Why is this important for your target audience?"

**Response:**
"Modern gamers increasingly expect personalization and immersion. Studies show that younger players, especially Gen Z, prefer voice interfaces—they grew up with Siri and Alexa. Additionally, the RPG market is growing at 13% annually, and players are seeking innovation beyond graphics improvements. By addressing these four limitations, we're not just fixing technical problems; we're meeting evolving player expectations for how they want to interact with game worlds."

### If asked: "What about players who don't want to use voice?"

**Response:**
"Great point. Our system is multi-modal by design. Players can choose voice input, text input, or hybrid. The AI processes both identically, so no player is forced into one interaction mode. This actually addresses accessibility from both angles: voice-preferred users AND text-preferred users both get full functionality."

## PRACTICE TIPS

### Before Presentation
1. **Record yourself:** Practice this script on camera, watch back for:
   - Pacing (too fast/slow?)
   - Clarity (mumbling specific words?)
   - Body language (natural or stiff?)
   
2. **Time yourself:** Aim for 2:30-3:00 minutes exactly
   - If over 3:15, cut some examples
   - If under 2:15, slow down or add emphasis pauses

3. **Practice transitions:** The move from this slide to next should be seamless

4. **Memorize key phrases:**
   - "multiple-choice questionnaire"
   - "museum effect"
   - "quest vending machines"
   - "talk to our refrigerators"
   
5. **Prepare backup examples:** In case audience doesn't know your references

### During Presentation
1. **Watch for audience reactions:**
   - Nodding = they agree, keep pace
   - Confused faces = slow down, clarify
   - Looking at phones = speed up or add energy

2. **Adjust on the fly:**
   - If running long, skip the "sweeping for 8 hours" joke
   - If audience seems technical, add more depth to "disconnected systems"

3. **Use the slide as backup:**
   - If you forget a point, the bullet points are your safety net
   - Glance at slide, don't read from it
